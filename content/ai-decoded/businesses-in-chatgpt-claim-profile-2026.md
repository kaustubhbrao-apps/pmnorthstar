For two years the frustrating thing about AI search was that you couldn't *do* anything about it. Google gives businesses a profile to claim; ChatGPT gave you a black box. On November 10, 2026, that started to change: OpenAI launched **"Businesses in ChatGPT,"** a beta that lets companies **verify a profile and, in some cases, run a 'business agent'** inside the assistant. It's small, it's limited, and it's the first real surface for brand presence in an answer engine.

## What launched

Per reporting on the announcement:

- **A claim-and-verify flow.** Eligible companies can claim a business profile; verified ones get a **blue verification badge** meant to show an agent genuinely represents the business.
- **Verified business agents.** Some businesses can run an agent inside ChatGPT, available first to **eligible US e-commerce and online-service companies.**
- **Analytics.** Claimed profiles get stats on **mentions in ChatGPT, website visits, and the topics through which users discover the business.**

Two honest caveats. OpenAI **hasn't fully disclosed the verification process** (reported options include phone, email, Shopify, or domain). And verification **does not make ChatGPT recommend you more** — the company has said so directly. This is a presence-and-measurement surface, not a ranking lever.

## Why a limited beta is still a big deal

Because it's the **"Google Business Profile" moment for AI search** — the point where an answer engine stops being a pure black box and gives brands a handle to grab.

[We've argued the SEO funnel moved inside the model](/ai-decoded/death-of-seo-funnel-ai-search-2026): the consideration phase now happens in the answer, where you had no analytics and no identity. "Businesses in ChatGPT" cracks that open on two fronts:

- **Identity.** You can assert, verifiably, that this is your brand — the groundwork for trust signals, and eventually richer presence, inside answers.
- **Measurement.** For the first time you can see how often ChatGPT mentions you and which topics surface you. That's the single hardest metric in [generative engine optimization](/answers/what-is-generative-engine-optimization) to track, and it just moved from "manual sampling" to "dashboard."

Note the precise definition OpenAI reportedly uses: for an online business, a **"mention" counts when ChatGPT references your website** — your brand name alone doesn't count. That detail tells you what to optimize for: being the cited *source*, not just a name that comes up.

## What to do about it

- **Claim and verify early** if you're eligible. It's cheap, it secures your identity, and it unlocks the analytics — which is the real prize.
- **Treat the mentions metric as your new north star for AI visibility.** Watch which topics surface you and which don't; the gaps are your content roadmap.
- **Don't expect a ranking boost.** Keep doing the work that actually earns citations — original data, citable claims, structural clarity, brand presence across the wider corpus — because that's still what gets you *into* the answer. Verification just lets you *measure and own* your place in it.
- **Expect this surface to grow.** A badge and basic stats today is how these platforms always start. The brands that claim and learn the metrics now will be fluent when the surface gets richer.

## The takeaway

"Businesses in ChatGPT" won't move your recommendations tomorrow, and that's fine — that's not what it's for yet. Its real value is that **the black box finally has a door.** You can claim your identity and, for the first time, measure your presence inside an answer engine. In an era where the funnel lives inside the model, the first brands to instrument their visibility there will have a head start on everyone still guessing. Claim the profile; watch the mentions; keep earning the citation.
