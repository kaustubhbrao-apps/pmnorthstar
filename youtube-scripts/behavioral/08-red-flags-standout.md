[VISUAL: Header: "THE 6 BEHAVIORAL RED FLAGS." Six red-flag slots fill one at a time.]

EV: Red flag one — *blaming others*. In any story about failure or conflict, if you point the finger — "the engineers were slow, my manager was clueless, marketing dropped the ball" — you reveal someone who won't take ownership. It's the single most damaging pattern. Own your part in *everything*, even when others shared the blame. Accountability is non-negotiable.

[VISUAL: Red flag 2 — a "we we we" cloud hiding the individual.]

EV: Red flag two — the *endless "we"*. When you describe your work entirely as "we did this, we did that," the interviewer can't tell what *you* actually contributed. It can sound like you're *hiding* a lack of personal impact. Use "I" for your specific actions — "*I* led this, *I* decided that" — while still crediting the team. If they can't find *you* in your own story, that's a problem.

[VISUAL: Red flag 3 — a rambling, endless story with no point.]

EV: Red flag three — *rambling with no structure*. A story that wanders forever, buries the point, and never lands a result. It signals poor communication — a core PM skill — and it exhausts the interviewer. This is exactly what STAR prevents. Be crisp, hit the beats, land the result. A great story told as a swamp scores like a weak one.

[VISUAL: Red flag 4 — a fake failure ("I work too hard") with a nose growing.]

EV: Red flag four — *fake failures and zero self-awareness*. "My biggest weakness is caring too much." Interviewers see right through it, and it signals you either lack self-awareness or won't be honest. Give a *real* failure, owned, with a lesson. The ability to honestly reflect on your flaws is a *strength* they're actively looking for — don't rob yourself of it with a cardboard answer.

[VISUAL: Red flag 5 — a candidate who steamrolled or bulldozed, framed as a 'win'.]

EV: Red flag five — *steamrolling as a flex*. Telling a conflict story where you "won" by bulldozing someone, thinking it shows strength. It shows you're *hard to work with*. PMs influence and align; they don't bully. Pick stories that show collaboration and mature resolution, not conquest. "I was right and I overruled everyone" is a red flag, not a highlight.

[VISUAL: Red flag 6 — a disengaged candidate with no questions, no energy.]

EV: And red flag six — *disengagement*. No questions at the end, flat energy, generic answers that could apply to any company, no evident enthusiasm for *this* role. It signals you don't really care, and interviewers hire people who *want* to be there. Show genuine interest, bring energy, ask real questions. Indifference loses to enthusiasm every time, even with equal skill.

EV: Dodge those six — blaming, endless "we," rambling, fake failures, steamrolling, and disengagement — and you've cleared most of the bar. But we're not here for "most." Let's talk about the moves that make you *unforgettable*.

[VISUAL: Header: "THE 5 STANDOUT MOVES." Five gold stars light up.]

EV: Standout move one — *radical ownership*. Go beyond not-blaming — actively take ownership, even of things that weren't fully your fault. "The launch failed, and while a lot went wrong, *I* was the PM, so I own it — here's what I'd do differently." That level of accountability is rare and magnetic; it's what leaders sound like. Move two — *quantified impact*. Whenever possible, attach a *number* to your results — "lifted conversion twelve percent," "cut churn by a third." Numbers make your stories credible and memorable, and most candidates speak only in vague qualities. Be the one with receipts.

[VISUAL: Gold star 3 lights — self-aware reflection; a star 4 — genuine passion; a star 5 — tailored, specific-to-them answers.]

EV: Move three — *genuine self-awareness*. Reflect honestly on what you've learned and how you've grown. "I used to over-index on shipping fast; I've learned to balance speed with validation." That maturity signals someone who'll keep *growing*, which is exactly who teams want to bet on. Move four — *authentic passion*. Let real enthusiasm show — for product, for the mission, for *this* company specifically. Energy is contagious and it's rare; the candidate who clearly *cares* lifts the whole room. And move five — *specificity and tailoring*. Generic answers are forgettable; specific, tailored ones stick. Reference *this* company's product, connect your stories to *this* role, ask questions about *their* actual situation. The candidate who clearly did their homework and made it personal is the one remembered after fifty interviews.

EV: Let me leave you with a prep checklist you can start *today*, because behavioral rounds reward preparation more than almost anything. One — build your *story bank*: five to seven rich, real stories covering leadership, conflict, failure, influence, success, and ambiguity. Two — *structure each in STAR*, plus that reflection beat, and practice them *out loud* until they're crisp and under two minutes. Three — for each story, *quantify the result* with a real number. Run that checklist and you walk in *prepared*, not winging it — which, remember, is the single biggest differentiator in behavioral rounds.
