EV: "So, do you have any questions for me?" Most candidates treat this as the interview being *over*. Huge mistake — because you're still being tested, and this is one of your biggest chances to shine *and* to figure out if you even want the job. Today, the questions to ask. Let's go.

[VISUAL: pmnorthstar logo pops. Title card: "QUESTIONS TO ASK THEM."]

EV: I'm EV, from pmnorthstar. That moment at the end — "any questions for me?" — is wildly underrated. Candidates relax, mumble "nope, I'm good," or ask something they could've Googled, and they *waste* it. But the questions you ask are a *signal* — they reveal how you think, what you care about, and how serious you are. Plus, remember — an interview is a *two-way* street; you're evaluating *them* too. So this video is about asking questions that both impress *and* genuinely help you decide. Let's go.

[VISUAL: Header: "WHY THIS MOMENT MATTERS." Two arrows: 'impresses them' + 'informs you'.]

EV: First, why this matters so much — it does *double duty*. One — your questions *signal your quality*. Thoughtful, insightful questions show you think deeply about product, team, and strategy — they're a final impression that lingers. Two — they *genuinely inform your decision*. You're about to potentially spend years at this place; you *need* to know if it's a good fit, if the team's healthy, if you'll grow. So great questions aren't a performance *for* them — they're a real tool *for you* that *also* happens to impress. Never waste this moment. And never, ever say "no, I don't have any" — that signals disinterest and is a genuine red flag.

[VISUAL: Header: "THE 4 CATEGORIES OF GREAT QUESTIONS." Four themed buckets.]

EV: So what should you ask? Four categories of great questions — pick a few that *you* genuinely care about. Let's walk them.

[VISUAL: Bucket 1 — questions about the role and team.]

EV: Category one — *the role and the team*. These show you're thinking seriously about actually *doing* the job. "What does success look like for this role in the first six months?" "How does the product team work with engineering and design here?" "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?" That last one is great — it gets a *real*, unscripted answer and shows you want to know what you'd actually be walking into. These questions are practical, grounded, and signal you're already picturing yourself in the seat.

[VISUAL: Bucket 2 — questions about product strategy and decisions.]

EV: Category two — *product strategy and decisions*. These show you think like a *product person*, not just an employee. "How are product decisions made here — how do you balance data with intuition?" "What's the product strategy for the next year, and what's the thinking behind it?" "How do you decide what *not* to build?" These signal that you care about the *craft* of product and that you think strategically. They also give you genuinely useful intel on whether this is a place that makes *good* product decisions — or a chaotic one.

[VISUAL: Bucket 3 — questions about growth and culture.]

EV: Category three — *growth and culture*. These help *you* decide if you'll thrive and grow. "How do PMs grow and develop here?" "What's the feedback culture like?" "What do you personally enjoy most about working here — and what's hard about it?" That last one is wonderful, because asking the *interviewer* about their own experience is personal, disarming, and gets you an *honest* read on the culture. These questions show you care about *long-term* fit, not just landing any job — and they protect *you* from a bad environment.

[VISUAL: Quiet lower-third: "Subscribe to pmnorthstar."]

EV: Quick nudge — if this is useful, subscribe to pmnorthstar so you catch the finale. One category left, and it's the sharpest.

[VISUAL: Bucket 4 — a question that builds on something discussed in the interview.]

EV: Category four — and this is the *power move* — a question that *builds on something from the conversation itself*. Instead of a generic pre-written question, you reference something the interviewer said earlier: "Earlier you mentioned the team's pivoting toward enterprise — I'm curious, how is that changing how you prioritize?" This is the *most* impressive kind, because it proves you were *genuinely listening and engaged*, it's authentic rather than canned, and it creates a real conversation. So always keep a mental note during the interview of things you'd love to dig into, and bring one back at the end. A tailored, in-the-moment question beats any generic one — it shows you were *present*, not just waiting for your turn.

[VISUAL: Header: "WHAT *NOT* TO ASK." Red-flag questions crossed out.]

EV: And the questions to *avoid*. Don't ask things you could easily Google or find on their website — "so what does your company do?" signals you didn't even prepare. Early on, be a little careful leading hard with *only* salary, vacation, and perks — those matter and there's a right time, but if your *very first* questions are all about what *you* get and nothing about the *work*, it can signal misplaced priorities. And don't ask something *so* softball it's obviously just to have a question — interviewers can tell. The test is simple: ask what you *genuinely* want to know to decide if this job is right for you. Authentic curiosity always reads better than a performance.

[VISUAL: Header: "KEEP A FEW IN YOUR POCKET." A small set of go-to questions ready.]

EV: One practical tip — prepare a *small set* of go-to questions in advance across these categories, so you're never caught empty — but stay ready to *swap* one for a better, in-the-moment question that comes up naturally. Three or four solid questions is plenty; you won't have time for more. And here's a bonus — the *answers* you get are genuinely valuable data. If you ask "what's the hardest part of working here?" and get a nervous, evasive non-answer, that tells you something important. You're interviewing *them* as much as they're interviewing you. Use it.

[VISUAL: Recap: the four question categories, with the 'build on the conversation' one glowing brightest.]

EV: So — questions to ask them. This moment is still part of the test *and* a real tool for your decision — never waste it, never say "no questions." Ask across four categories: the role and team, product strategy, growth and culture, and — the power move — a question that builds on something said in the interview. Avoid the Googleable and the purely self-interested. Prepare a few, stay flexible, and treat their answers as data about *them*. Nail this, and you leave a final, lasting impression of a thoughtful, engaged product thinker.

[VISUAL: Header: "READ THEIR ANSWERS — IT GOES BOTH WAYS." The candidate interpreting the interviewer's responses as data.]

EV: Let me close with the half of this that candidates *completely* miss — the *answers you get are data about whether you even want this job*. You're so focused on impressing them that you forget to actually *listen* to what their answers reveal. So pay attention. Ask "what's the hardest part of working here?" — if they give a thoughtful, honest answer, that's a healthy, self-aware culture; if they freeze, deflect, or nervously say "oh, nothing really," that evasiveness tells you something's off. Ask "how are product decisions made?" — if the answer is "the loudest executive usually wins," you've just learned you'd be fighting politics every day. Ask "how do PMs grow here?" — a vague non-answer suggests they haven't thought about *your* development at all. See what's happening? Every question does double duty — it signals your quality *to them*, and their answer reveals the truth *to you*. You might be so eager for an offer that you ignore giant red flags waving in their responses — don't. A job is a two-year-plus commitment of your life; interview *them* as hard as they interview you. The best candidates walk out not just having impressed, but genuinely *knowing* whether this is a place they'd thrive. That clarity protects your career — so listen to the answers as carefully as you craft your questions.
