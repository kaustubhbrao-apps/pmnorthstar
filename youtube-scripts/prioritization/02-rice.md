[VISUAL: pmnorthstar logo pops. Title card: "RICE."]

EV: I'm EV, from pmnorthstar. Last video we learned prioritization is the core PM skill, and that frameworks are tools to structure our judgment. Today, the most famous tool of all — RICE. It takes a fuzzy gut feeling about a feature and breaks it into four honest questions, giving you a *score* you can compare across options. It's beloved because it forces you to be specific about *why* something matters. Let's break down each letter.

[VISUAL: Header: "R · REACH." A crowd of users; a portion highlighted.]

EV: R is for Reach. The question: *how many people will this affect, in a given time period?* Not "is this important" — literally, how many users does it touch per quarter, per month? A feature that affects every single user has way more reach than one that helps a tiny niche. Be concrete: "this hits all two hundred thousand monthly users" versus "this helps maybe five hundred power users." Reach keeps you honest about scale — because it's easy to fall in love with a feature that, realistically, almost no one will ever see.

[VISUAL: Header: "I · IMPACT." A user with a small nudge vs a big transformation.]

EV: I is for Impact. The question: *when it does affect someone, how much does it help them?* This is about depth, where Reach was about breadth. Does this feature massively improve the experience, or is it a tiny nice-to-have? RICE usually scores Impact on a simple scale — like three for massive, two for high, one for medium, down to a quarter for minimal. "This transforms the core workflow" is high impact; "this changes a button color" is low. Reach times Impact already tells you a lot — lots of people, helped a lot, is the dream.

[VISUAL: Header: "C · CONFIDENCE." A meter swinging from "hunch" to "hard data."]

EV: C is for Confidence — and this one's the secret sauce that keeps you grounded. The question: *how sure are you about your Reach and Impact estimates?* Because let's be honest, sometimes those numbers are backed by real data, and sometimes they're hopeful guesses. Confidence is a percentage — a hundred percent if you've got solid evidence, maybe eighty if you're fairly sure, fifty if it's a genuine gamble. It's a humility multiplier. It stops you from ranking a wild, unproven moonshot above a safe, proven win just because the moonshot's *imagined* numbers look huge. Confidence forces you to discount your own hype.

[VISUAL: Header: "E · EFFORT." A clock / person-months counter ticking up.]

EV: And E is for Effort. The question: *how much work will this take?* Usually measured in person-months — how many people, for how long. And here's the crucial bit — Effort goes in the *denominator*. It *divides* the score. Because a feature that delivers big value for tiny effort should rank way above one that delivers the same value for enormous effort. Effort is what turns RICE from "what's most valuable" into "what's most valuable *per unit of work*" — which is what you actually care about when resources are tight.

[VISUAL: Header: "THE FORMULA." (R × I × C) ÷ E assembles with a satisfying snap, producing a score.]

EV: So here's the whole formula, and it's beautifully simple. RICE score equals Reach, times Impact, times Confidence — all divided by Effort. Reach times Impact times Confidence gives you the *expected total value*. Divide by Effort and you get *value per unit of work*. Score every feature this way, rank them high to low, and you've got a prioritized list that's grounded in four honest questions instead of whoever argued loudest. That's the magic — it makes your reasoning *visible and comparable*.

[VISUAL: Header: "WATCH ME SCORE TWO FEATURES." Two feature cards get RICE scores side by side.]

EV: Let me score two features head-to-head so you see it work. Feature A — a flashy new dashboard redesign. Reach: all users, high. Impact: medium, it's nicer but not transformative, call it two. Confidence: eighty percent. Effort: big — four person-months. Feature B — a quick fix to a broken checkout button. Reach: everyone who checks out, high. RICE just told us to fix the checkout before polishing the dashboard — which is obviously right, but now we can *prove* it to anyone who pushes back. That's the power.

[VISUAL: Header, cautionary: "RICE PITFALLS." Warning icons.]

EV: Now, three pitfalls to use RICE wisely. Pitfall one — false precision. RICE spits out a specific number, which can trick you into thinking it's *objective*. It's not — your inputs are still estimates. Use the score to *compare and discuss*, not as gospel. Pitfall two — forgetting Confidence. Candidates often skip it, and then a fantasy feature with made-up huge numbers rockets to the top. Confidence is what keeps you honest — never drop it. And pitfall three — RICE doesn't capture everything. It misses things like strategic value, dependencies, or a feature that *unblocks* future work. So use RICE as your starting rank, then apply judgment for the factors it can't see. Tool, not oracle — remember?

[VISUAL: Recap: the R-I-C-E letters with the formula (R×I×C)÷E glowing.]

EV: So — RICE. Reach, how many people. Impact, how much it helps each. Confidence, how sure you are. Effort, how much work — and it divides. Score equals Reach times Impact times Confidence, over Effort. It turns gut feelings into comparable numbers you can defend in any room. Just respect its limits — watch the false precision, never skip Confidence, and layer judgment for what it misses.

[VISUAL: Header: "WHY CONFIDENCE IS THE UNSUNG HERO." A flashy moonshot with huge fake numbers vs a humble proven win; confidence multiplier flips the ranking.]

EV: Let me dwell on Confidence for a second, because it's the letter everyone underrates and it's secretly the one that saves you from disaster. Picture two features. Feature X is a wild, exciting moonshot — "this could *ten-x* our growth." Reach: everyone. Impact: three, massive. On those numbers alone, it rockets to the top. But Confidence? Honestly... maybe twenty percent — it's a total gamble, no evidence, just hope. Feature Y is a boring, proven improvement. Reach: solid. Impact: two. But Confidence: a hundred percent — we've tested it, we *know* it works. Watch what Confidence does: it multiplies X's dreamy score down by eighty percent, while Y keeps its full value. Suddenly the "boring" sure-thing outranks the sexy gamble — which is *exactly right* most of the time, because a bird in the hand beats a fantasy in the bush. Without Confidence, teams chase shiny moonshots and starve the reliable wins. With it, you stay honest about the difference between what you *know* and what you merely *hope*. That's why I never let a candidate skip the C.
