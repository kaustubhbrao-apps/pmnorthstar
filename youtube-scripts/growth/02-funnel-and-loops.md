EV: I'm EV, from pmnorthstar. To grow a product, you need a mental model of the *whole journey* a user takes — from never hearing of you, to loving you and bringing their friends. The classic model is a funnel, and the most famous one has a hilarious name — AARRR, the "pirate metrics." Then I'll show you why the smartest companies have evolved past funnels into *loops*. Let's start with the pirate.

[VISUAL: Header: "AARRR — THE PIRATE FUNNEL." Five stages stack: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue.]

EV: AARRR stands for the five stages every user passes through: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, and Revenue. Yes, the initials spell the pirate noise — that's literally why it stuck. Let me walk all five, because this is the backbone of the whole series.

[VISUAL: Each stage lights up as EV describes it, with a quick icon.]

EV: Stage one — *Acquisition*: how do people first *find* you? Ads, search, social, word of mouth. Getting strangers to show up. Stage two — *Activation*: do they have a great *first experience* and reach the "aha" moment where they get the value? This is where most products quietly lose people. Stage three — *Retention*: do they *come back*? Do they keep using it over time, or try it once and vanish? Stage four — *Referral*: do they *tell others*, bringing new users in? And stage five — *Revenue*: do they, or the business, *make money* from them? Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue. Five stages, a complete picture of turning a stranger into a paying, loyal, evangelizing user.

[VISUAL: Header: "THE ORDER IS THE INSIGHT." A spotlight on Retention, out of sequence, with "fix me first" glowing.]

EV: Now here's the deep insight most people miss — the *order* matters, but not the way you'd think. Instinct says start at the top: pour energy into Acquisition, get tons of users. But that's the classic trap. If your *Retention* is broken — if users leave as fast as they arrive — then acquiring more is like pouring water into a leaky bucket. You spend and spend and the level never rises. So the counterintuitive pro move: often you should fix the *middle* of the funnel — Activation and Retention — *before* you pour money into the top. Get people to stick *first*, then turn on acquisition. There's even a reordering some teams use — RARRA — that deliberately puts Retention *first* to make exactly this point. Don't fill a leaky bucket.

[VISUAL: Header: "WHERE'S THE BIGGEST LEAK?" The funnel with drop-offs at each stage; one stage leaks dramatically.]

EV: And here's how you actually use the funnel to grow — find the *biggest leak*. Map your numbers at each stage: say a hundred thousand people discover you, twenty thousand activate, five thousand retain. Now look — where's the steepest drop-off? If you lose eighty percent between Acquisition and Activation, your onboarding is the leak, and fixing *that* will do more than any amount of extra ads. The funnel turns "how do we grow?" into a precise "which stage is leaking most, and how do we fix *that one*?" It's a diagnostic tool — exactly like segmentation in root-cause analysis. Find the biggest leak, plug it, repeat.

[VISUAL: Header: "FUNNELS vs LOOPS." A linear funnel (one-way, ends) vs a circular loop (feeds itself).]

EV: Now the big upgrade — growth *loops*. Here's the limitation of a funnel: it's *linear*. Users enter the top, flow down, and... it ends. To grow more, you have to keep pouring more in at the top, usually by spending more money. A loop is different — it's *circular*. The *output* of the loop feeds back as the *input* to the next cycle, so growth *compounds* on itself without you constantly paying. Funnels leak and end; loops feed themselves. This is how the fastest-growing products in history actually grew.

[VISUAL: A loop animates: user creates value → attracts new user → who creates value → attracts more.]

EV: Let me make loops concrete with the main types. A *viral loop*: a user invites friends to get a benefit, those friends join and invite *their* friends — each user generates more users. A *content loop*: users create content, that content gets found by search engines, new users discover it, sign up, and create *more* content that attracts even more people. A *paid loop*: you acquire a user, they generate revenue, you reinvest that revenue to acquire *more* users — this works as long as each user earns you more than they cost. The magic of all three: each turn of the loop makes the *next* turn bigger. That's compounding growth, and it's why loop-driven companies can explode while funnel-only companies have to keep buying every single user.

[VISUAL: Header: "HOW IT SHOWS UP IN INTERVIEWS." A growth question answered with funnel + loop thinking.]

EV: So how does this win in interviews? When asked "how would you grow this product?", don't just say "run more ads." Think in both frames. Funnel: "First I'd map the AARRR funnel and find the biggest leak — if activation's weak, fixing onboarding beats buying more users." Loop: "Then I'd look for a growth loop we could build — is there a natural way users bring other users, or create content that pulls people in? Because a loop compounds, while ads are a treadmill." Showing you think in *loops*, not just funnels, instantly signals sophisticated growth thinking. It's the difference between someone who'd rent growth and someone who'd *build* it.

[VISUAL: Recap: the AARRR funnel beside a self-feeding loop, both glowing.]

EV: So — the funnel and loops. AARRR: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue — the full journey, and remember, fix the leaky middle before pouring into the top. Find the biggest leak and plug it. Then level up to loops — viral, content, paid — where the output feeds the input and growth *compounds*. Funnels to diagnose, loops to compound. That's the growth mindset.

[VISUAL: Header: "WATCH A LOOP OUT-RUN A FUNNEL." Two products race: funnel one plateaus, loop one curves upward and compounds.]

EV: Let me show you *why* loops are so magical with a quick head-to-head, because the difference compounds into something staggering. Product A grows with a pure *funnel* — they buy users with ads. To double their growth, they have to double their ad spend. Linear. Expensive. The moment the money stops, the growth stops flat. Product B grows with a *loop* — every user they get brings, say, half a new user through referrals or content. Watch what happens: a hundred users bring fifty, who bring twenty-five, who bring twelve... and meanwhile each *new* batch *also* loops. The growth *curves upward* and compounds on itself, for free, while Product A is still writing checks for every single user. Give it a year, and the loop-driven product has left the funnel-driven one in the dust — not because they spent more, but because their growth *multiplied* while the other's just *added*. That's the entire reason the fastest-growing companies in history were loop-driven. So in any growth question, the funnel helps you *diagnose*, but always, always ask: "where's our *loop*?" Because a funnel is a cost, and a loop is an *engine*.
