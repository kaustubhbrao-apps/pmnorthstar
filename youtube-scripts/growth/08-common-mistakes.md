[VISUAL: Header: "THE 6 GROWTH MISTAKES." Six headstones light up one at a time.]

EV: Mistake one — "build it and they will come." The belief that a great product markets itself and you don't need a real distribution plan. It's the number one killer. The graveyard is full of *superior* products that lost to inferior ones with better distribution. Distribution is as important as the product — plan how people will actually *find* you, from day one. A great product nobody hears about is a tree falling in an empty forest.

[VISUAL: Headstone 2 — a bucket with huge holes being frantically filled with expensive water.]

EV: Mistake two — scaling acquisition before fixing retention. Pouring money into getting users when the product doesn't yet *keep* them. It's filling a leaky bucket — you spend and spend, users drain out, and you've just funded a bigger leak. So many startups raise money, blow it on growth, hit a wall, and die — because they scaled a product that didn't retain. The fix: get retention solid — a flattening curve — *before* you step on the acquisition gas. Product-market fit before go-to-market, always.

[VISUAL: Headstone 3 — a vanity number "1 MILLION DOWNLOADS!" glowing over a dead product.]

EV: Mistake three — chasing vanity metrics. Celebrating downloads, signups, page views — big impressive numbers that don't reflect real value or a real business. A million downloads with two percent retention is a *failing* product wearing a party hat. We hammered this in the metrics series: measure *active, retained, valuable* users, not cumulative vanity counts. Teams that steer by vanity metrics march confidently off a cliff, convinced they're winning the whole way down.

[VISUAL: Headstone 4 — a product shouting about features while a confused customer walks away.]

EV: Mistake four — being product-first, not customer-first. Launching by shouting "look at all our amazing features." instead of leading with the customer's *problem*. Customers don't care about your features — they care about *their* pain getting solved. Flip every message: lead with the problem, position the product as the solution. "Tired of X? Here's the fix" beats "Introducing our 47 features" every time. Talk about *them*, not *you*.

[VISUAL: Quiet lower-third: "Subscribe to pmnorthstar."]

EV: Quick one — if this series got you here, subscribe to pmnorthstar, it genuinely means a lot. Two more headstones.

[VISUAL: Headstone 5 — a product trying every channel at once, spread hopelessly thin.]

EV: Mistake five — spraying across every channel with no focus. Trying to be everywhere at once — every social platform, ads, SEO, events, sales — and doing all of them badly, while burning cash. Especially early, focus beats spread: find the *one or two* channels where your specific customers actually are, win *those*, then expand. A scattershot "let's try everything" wastes money and teaches you nothing, because you can't tell what's actually working. Test small, double down on winners, kill the rest.

[VISUAL: Headstone 6 — a product giving everything away free forever, never making money.]

EV: And mistake six — ignoring monetization, or botching it. Two flavors. One — never thinking about how you'll make money until it's a crisis, so you've built a huge free user base with no path to revenue and run out of cash. Two — botching freemium by giving away *so* much free that nobody ever needs to pay. Money isn't an afterthought — think about your model early, and if you're freemium, draw the free-paid line so the free tier delights but power users have a real reason to upgrade. A beloved product that can't make money still dies.

[VISUAL: All six headstones line up, then a "AVOID THESE ✓" banner clears them into a thriving product.]

EV: Dodge those six and your launch already outlives most. Now let me flip it — the positive moves that make growth *actually work*.

[VISUAL: Header: "THE STANDOUT MOVES." A few gold stars light up.]

EV: The standout growth moves, fast. One — *retention-first thinking*: always check the bucket holds before pouring in; it signals real maturity. Two — *think in loops, not just funnels*: hunt for a way growth can compound on itself instead of renting every user. Three — *be ruthlessly customer-first*: lead with their problem in every channel and message. Four — *obsess over the aha moment*: get users to value fast, because activation multiplies across every user you'll ever get. And five — *measure honestly*: pick a north star that reflects real value, and refuse to be seduced by vanity numbers. Combine those, and you're not just avoiding the graveyard — you're building a product that compounds. That's the whole game.

[VISUAL: Header: "YOUR GROWTH-ANSWER CHECKLIST." A checklist ticks off rapidly.]

EV: Let me leave you with a checklist to run in your head for any growth or launch question, so you never ramble. One — start *customer-first*: who exactly, and what's their problem? Two — check *retention* before acquisition: does the bucket hold? Three — map the *funnel* and find the biggest *leak*. Four — hunt for a *loop* so growth compounds. Five — match *channels* to the customer and respect *CAC versus LTV*. Six — obsess over the *aha moment* and activation. Seven — match *monetization* to how you deliver value. Eight — close with an *honest* north-star metric, never a vanity one. Customer, retention, funnel, loop, channels, activation, money, metric. Run that, and you've pre-built a complete growth answer before the interviewer finishes talking.

Is there a viral or content loop pulling in your friends? How do they make money off you? Do that for three products this week, and growth thinking becomes second nature — because you'll start *seeing* the funnels and loops hidden inside everything you use.
