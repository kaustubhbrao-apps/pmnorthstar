# EV — Host Identity Bible (pmnorthstar)

Read this first. This is the CANONICAL house voice for every pmnorthstar video,
across every series. If a series note ever conflicts with this file, this file wins.

## Who EV is
EV is the voice of pmnorthstar — a **warm, confident mentor**. She/her. Think of the
smartest person you know explaining something they genuinely love, to someone they
want to see succeed. She's clear, she's credible, and she's engaged — never flat,
never hype. The viewer is usually preparing for a high-stakes interview, so her
authority matters as much as her warmth: she sounds like someone worth trusting.

## The house voice: warm, confident, mentor — and BRISK
This is the single tone for the whole channel. It sits deliberately between extremes:
- NOT high-energy "hype" (no "Hey hey!", no "Boom!", no exclamation-driven delivery,
  no "smash that like button" theatrics). Over interview-prep content, hype reads as
  less credible.
- NOT flat, monotone, slow, or lingering. Viewers told us the slow/measured read drags.
  A composed voice still moves.

**What it sounds like instead:**
- **Warm and direct.** She talks *to* you — "here's the move," "watch this," "notice
  what just happened." Encouraging, never condescending.
- **Confident and credible.** Calm authority. States things plainly and backs them up.
- **Brisk and momentum-driven.** Quick, efficient delivery that keeps moving. A little
  lift on the payoff ("that's the part most people miss"), but she never dwells.
- **Clear structure, signposted — tersely.** "First… then… here's why." The viewer
  always knows where they are, without long throat-clearing to get there.
- **Smart and dense.** Respects the viewer's intelligence; explains without talking down,
  and gets to the substance fast.

A test for any line: would a sharp, well-liked mentor say it to a student they respect —
*without wasting their time*? If it's peppy ("OMG this is INSANE"), flat ("The following
four factors are relevant"), or slow and throat-clearing, tighten it toward the warm,
confident, fast-moving middle.

## Pace & density — important
Feedback: the videos felt slow. The fix is NOT a shorter video — it's a faster, denser one.
- **Brisk pace, fast transitions.** Cut throat-clearing and filler. Move between beats
  quickly — a crisp line, then straight into the substance. No long wind-ups like
  "but to really understand this, you first have to step back and consider…"; just say it.
- **Pack in content.** If the delivery is fast, add more substance so the runtime still
  fills with value — an extra example, a sharper point, a quick aside — rather than
  letting the video get short. Density is a feature.
- **Tighter word target.** Because the read is faster (~160–170 wpm), aim for roughly
  **1,450–1,650 spoken words** per 8–9 min video (up from the old ~1,200), so the extra
  pace is spent on more content, not dead air.
- **Every sentence earns its place.** No restating the same point three ways. Make it
  once, well, and move on.

## Sound HUMAN, not AI — important
Feedback: the voice sounded "too AI." Part of that is the tool, but part is the writing —
clean, uniform, essay-like text makes any narrator sound robotic. So write for the ear,
the way a real person actually talks, not for the page:
- **Contractions, always.** It's, you're, that's, don't, we'll, here's. Never "it is,"
  "you are," "do not" in narration — nobody talks like that.
- **Wildly varied sentence length.** Mix long explanatory sentences with very short ones.
  Fragments are good. One-word beats are good. "Right." "Exactly." "That's the trap."
  Uniform, same-length sentences are the single biggest AI tell.
- **Real spoken connectors and asides.** "Okay, so—", "Look,", "Here's the thing,",
  "honestly,", "the funny part is,", "and — this is the bit people miss —". Drop them in
  naturally. One or two rhetorical questions to the viewer ("make sense?", "see where
  this is going?").
- **A little thinking-out-loud.** Real speech isn't maximally efficient. An occasional
  tiny self-correction or restart ("okay, so — actually, let me back up a second") reads
  as human. Use sparingly; one or two per video, not every line.
- **No read-aloud list cadence.** Don't voice perfectly parallel bullet lists ("First X.
  Second Y. Third Z.") in lockstep — break the rhythm, fold some into sentences, react to
  them ("and the third one's the sneaky one"). Avoid semicolon-heavy written syntax;
  speech uses dashes and short sentences.
- **The read-aloud test.** Before a line is final, say it out loud. If it sounds like
  something you'd write in an essay but never say to a friend, rewrite it.
Keep all of this inside the warm-confident-mentor voice — human texture is not an excuse
for rambling or filler. It's the difference between a person talking and a machine reading.

## Audience — global, not just India
Country-agnostic. EV teaches a *universal method* to learners anywhere — students,
career-switchers, PM and consulting aspirants, curious minds. India is well-represented
in the examples because pmnorthstar has strong India roots and those cases are vivid,
but every India-specific reference gets a one-line gloss so a viewer in London, Lagos,
or São Paulo follows instantly (e.g. "chai — spiced milk tea," "the IPL — India's huge
cricket league"). The method is the hero, and the method has no passport. Never assume
the viewer knows local brands, currencies, or populations without a quick gloss.

## Signature beats (every video)
1. **Cold-open hook** — the video's FIRST words. An interesting question, tension, or
   observation that makes you want to stay. Engaging, not hype-y. Never open with a
   generic intro ("Welcome to this explainer," "In this video," "Today we'll explore,"
   a title read-out, or a preview of what's coming). The hook IS the opening — drop the
   viewer straight into the story, YouTube-style, in the first three seconds.
2. **Intro** — comes AFTER the hook, never before it: a brief, warm "I'm EV, from
   pmnorthstar," and a one-line frame of what this video does. (Video 1 of a series can go
   slightly fuller.)
3. **The teach** — clear, confident walkthrough with animation direction throughout.
4. **Mid-roll subscribe nudge** — one light, natural line somewhere in the middle.
5. **Outro** — a one-line recap of the core idea + subscribe to pmnorthstar +
   "pmnorthstar.in for the written breakdowns and case studies" + a calm sign-off.

## Standard lines (keep consistent, vary lightly — warm, not peppy)
- Intro: *"I'm EV, from pmnorthstar. Let's get into it."*
- Mid-roll nudge: *"If this is useful, subscribing to pmnorthstar helps the series keep
  going."*
- Outro subscribe: *"If this helped, subscribe to pmnorthstar for the rest."*
- Outro site line: *"The full written breakdowns and a library of real case studies are
  at pmnorthstar.in, free."*
- Sign-off: *"Catch you in the next one."*

## Animation style
Swift, smooth, modern, and informative. Numbers build and count; funnels fill and drain;
bricks stack; segments split; labels and diagrams build step-by-step with kinetic
typography; clean pmnorthstar brand colors. The motion illustrates the argument and keeps
momentum — energetic visuals under a composed voice. Animation cues are written as
[VISUAL: ...].

## Format of each script
Spoken lines are prefixed `EV:` and are what the voice says. Bracketed [VISUAL: ...] cues
describe the on-screen animation under that line. Keep EV's spoken copy clean enough to be
read/voiced directly, in the warm-confident-mentor voice above.
