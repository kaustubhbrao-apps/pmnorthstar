EV: How many people watch the single biggest sports final in a cricket-mad country? We're talking hundreds of millions of humans. And we're going to funnel our way to the number — no data, just logic. Let's go.

[VISUAL: The dots pour into a giant funnel. pmnorthstar logo pops. Title card: "TOP-DOWN · EXAMPLE 1 · SPORTS FINAL."]

EV: Hey hey, EV here from pmnorthstar, and this is our first full top-down example — and it's a big one. The question: how many people watch the IPL final? Quick context for everyone outside India — the IPL is the Indian Premier League, India's absolutely enormous professional cricket league, and the final is basically the Super Bowl of cricket. If you're elsewhere, picture your country's biggest sporting event — a World Cup final, the Super Bowl. The funnel works for all of them. Let's run it.

[VISUAL: Four top-down framework cards flash in the corner: Anchor · Filters · Cascade · Check.]

EV: Remember our four top-down steps — pick the anchor, build the filters, cascade down, sanity-check. This is a textbook funnel, because we've got a giant, rock-solid anchor and the whole job is filtering it down to the slice that actually watches. Watch.

[VISUAL: Header: "STEP ZERO · SCOPE IT." Question bubbles pop.]

EV: Step zero — scope. I'd ask: are we counting people who watch *any meaningful part* of the final, across *all* channels — television plus streaming — or just one medium? Let's count total unique viewers across TV and streaming who catch a real chunk of the match. And we mean the *final* specifically — the single biggest match of the season, not an average league game. And all of India. Scope locked. These distinctions matter — "watched any part on any screen" is a very different number from "watched the whole thing on TV."

[VISUAL: Header: "STEP 1 · THE ANCHOR." A giant "1.4 BILLION" glows at the top of the funnel.]

EV: Step one — the anchor. India's population: about one point four billion people. That's the top of our funnel, and it's a number we're rock-solid confident in. Perfect anchor. Now we filter down.

[VISUAL: Header: "STEP 2 · BUILD & APPLY THE FILTERS." The funnel appears with three empty layers, ready to fill.]

EV: Step two — the filters. And the discipline here is to use just a few strong, distinct ones. I'm going to use exactly three. Watch the number cascade.

[VISUAL: Filter 1 layer fills: "CRICKET INTEREST · 60% → 840M." A cricket bat icon.]

EV: Filter one — cricket interest. Not everyone in India follows cricket, though the fraction is famously huge. Say sixty percent of the population has at least some interest and would plausibly watch a big match. One point four billion times sixty percent is eight hundred forty million. And I'd say that out loud — "sixty percent, because cricket's close to a national religion here but we should still carve out the genuinely uninterested."

[VISUAL: Filter 2 layer fills: "SCREEN ACCESS · 75% → 630M." A TV + smartphone icon.]

EV: Filter two — access to a screen. To watch, you need a TV, or a smartphone with a data connection, available during the match. Across urban and rural India combined, say seventy-five percent of the cricket-interested crowd have practical access to a live screen at that moment. Eight hundred forty million times seventy-five percent is six hundred thirty million.

[VISUAL: Filter 3 layer fills: "ACTUALLY TUNE IN · 40% → ~250M." A clock + "live" icon.]

EV: And filter three — do they actually tune into *this specific match* at *this specific time*? Having interest and a screen doesn't mean you're watching. Lots of people catch only highlights, or they're at work, or asleep if it runs late, or just don't tune in. The final's the marquee event so engagement is high — say forty percent of those with interest and access actually watch a meaningful part. Six hundred thirty million times forty percent is about two hundred fifty million.

[VISUAL: Header: "STEP 3 · READ THE BOTTOM." The funnel spout drips out a glowing "≈ 250 MILLION VIEWERS."]

EV: Step three — read off the bottom of the funnel. Out drips roughly two hundred fifty million viewers — twenty-five crore people — for the final. One match. A quarter of a billion humans. Let that sink in.

[VISUAL: Friendly lower-third: "👉 Subscribe to pmnorthstar." Pulse.]

EV: If funneling is feeling powerful right now, hit subscribe to pmnorthstar, it genuinely helps the channel grow. Now — does our number actually hold up?

[VISUAL: Header: "SANITY CHECK." Magnifying glass sweeps; a band appears "reported: 20–35 crore ✓".]

EV: Sanity check. Broadcasters and streaming platforms have publicly cited IPL final viewership in the range of twenty to thirty-five crore — two hundred to three hundred fifty million — across TV and digital combined in recent years, with record finals pushing the top end. Our two hundred fifty million sits comfortably right in the middle of that band. The funnel landed exactly in the right neighborhood — which is the whole goal. And remember: we didn't need to *know* the real figure. We *reasoned* our way to a defensible one. That's the entire skill.

[VISUAL: Header: "WHY EACH FILTER EARNED ITS PLACE." The three filters replay, each with a checkmark.]

EV: Now let me point out the discipline in that funnel, because it's a model for every top-down problem. We used exactly three filters — interest, access, actual tune-in — and each one captures a *genuinely distinct* reason a person would or wouldn't watch. We resisted the temptation to pile on more filters like "age" or "region," because those are already mostly baked into the interest and access fractions, and extra filters just compound your error. Three clean, non-overlapping cuts got us there. Fewer, stronger filters — remember that from the method video? This is it in action.

[VISUAL: Each fraction replays with its one-line reason as a caption.]

EV: And notice — I justified *every* fraction out loud. Sixty percent for interest, seventy-five for access, forty for tune-in — each with a one-sentence reason. The interviewer doesn't need you to be right about the exact fraction. They need to see you chose it *for a reason* instead of plucking it from the sky. That narration is the difference between a guess and a guesstimate.

[VISUAL: Header: "THE #1 MISTAKE HERE." A big red "100 CRORE?!" appears, then gets slashed.]

EV: And here's the classic mistake on this exact question, so don't fall for it. People conflate "cricket fans" with "people watching this one match." Someone goes, "India's got a billion cricket fans, so a billion watch the final" — and they've skipped the access and tune-in filters entirely, landing at a number bigger than the total number of screens in the country. The funnel exists precisely to stop that. Each layer strips away a group that *can't* or *doesn't* watch, so your final number reflects reality — not just national enthusiasm.

[VISUAL: Header: "THE FOLLOW-UP TO BE READY FOR." An ad-break icon and a ₹ symbol float up.]

EV: And be ready for the pivot, because it's a great one. The interviewer might go: "nice — now estimate the ad revenue of that final broadcast." And here's the beautiful part — that's just a *continuation* of the same funnel. You've got two hundred fifty million viewers. Now layer on: the broadcast runs maybe four hours, with some number of ad slots per hour, each slot priced at a premium given that massive audience. Or reason from the sponsor side — a handful of major sponsors each paying tens of crores, plus spot advertising. You'd walk from your viewership number, to an impressions number, to a revenue number. One guesstimate becomes the anchor for the next. And *that* chaining — where your answer to one question feeds the next — is exactly what senior product and strategy roles are really testing for.
