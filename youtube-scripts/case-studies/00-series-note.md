# Case Studies Series — Series Note | Hosted by EV

This series inherits the **pmnorthstar house voice** in full — warm, confident mentor,
brisk & dense. The canonical rules live in `../guesstimates/00-EV-host-bible.md`; that
file wins over anything here.

## What this series is
Ten "bomber" product case studies — celebratory, positive breakdowns of companies that
WON, each built around one transferable product lesson a PM or consulting candidate can
actually use in an interview. We're not here to roast anyone or play devil's advocate.
We admire the move, we explain *why* it worked, and we hand the viewer the principle.

These are high-pull topics on YouTube (big, loved, recognizable brands) with a PM lens —
so the title sells the brand, and the content delivers the framework.

## The slate
1. **Zomato** — profitability is a product problem (unit economics, Blinkit).
2. **Zerodha** — win by not spending; product as marketing, zero ad budget.
3. **Physics Wallah** — give genuine value away free, earn trust, then monetize affordably (disrupt from below).
4. **CRED** — who you say *no* to; exclusion as positioning.
5. **Netflix** — cannibalize your own cash cow (DVD → streaming) + recommendations.
6. **Spotify** — make the product market itself (Discover Weekly, Wrapped).
7. **Duolingo** — retention *is* the growth strategy (streaks + owned channels).
8. **Airbnb** — do things that don't scale (the photography hack, design-led growth).
9. **Nike** — own the relationship (brand moat + DTC + first-party data).
10. **Amazon** — the flywheel; self-reinforcing growth loops.

## House rules for every script in this folder
- Open with a hook that sells the brand *and* a tension ("how did a company burning cash
  learn to print it?"), never hype. The hook is the FIRST thing said — no "welcome to
  this explainer," no "in this video," no title read-out. Cold open straight into the
  story, YouTube-style; "I'm EV, from pmnorthstar" comes after the hook, never before.
  (When generating in NotebookLM, paste the bible's delivery prompt so it doesn't add its
  own intro.)
- Teach ONE core product principle per video; name it, prove it with the company's real
  moves, then generalize it so it transfers.
- Positive framing throughout. We can name a hard moment (the cash-burn years, the near-
  death pivot) only as setup for the win — the arc always lands on what they got right.
- Global audience: gloss every India-specific reference in one line (IPL, lakh/crore,
  "₹ — Indian rupees") so a viewer in London or Lagos follows instantly.
- Land one interview takeaway per video ("in a case, you'd say…") — that's the pmnorthstar
  promise: entertaining to watch, useful in a room.
- **Each script carries a "WHERE THEY ARE NOW (2025–26)" beat** just before the lesson, folding
  in the company's current reality (rebrands, IPOs, regulation, strategy reversals) so nothing
  reads dated. Where the present complicates the story (e.g. Nike over-rotating on DTC and
  rebuilding wholesale), we tell it honestly and let the nuance *sharpen* the lesson rather than
  dodge it — staying net-positive on the company's arc.
- **Use each brand's iconic visual elements in the animation prompts.** Every script opens with a
  "Brand visual kit" line — the signature colors (hex), logo motif, and recognizable imagery for
  that company. The animation pass should lean on them hard so each video is unmistakably *that*
  brand and pops in a thumbnail/feed. Treat it as evocative homage for commentary and education:
  reference the real brand look, but never fake a partnership or endorsement, and never distort a
  logo in a way that misleads.
- Numbers are illustrative of the well-known public story; keep claims directional, not
  audited ("grew to tens of millions of users," "turned its first profit"), and never
  invent precise figures to sound authoritative.
- Target ~1,450–1,650 spoken words, 8–9 min, brisk pace, fast transitions. Density is the
  feature.
