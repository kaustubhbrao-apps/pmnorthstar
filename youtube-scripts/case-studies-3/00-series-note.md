## What this series is
Ten of the **highest-virality product stories in the world** — the ones with built-in drama,
huge recognition, and massive YouTube pull. This is the "best foot forward" series: the scripts
lean harder into story and the **[VISUAL:] direction is deliberately ambitious and cinematic** —
big, kinetic, crazy animation moments (a rocket landing itself, tokens streaming out of a chat
box, multiplayer cursors swarming a canvas, a GPU lighting up an AI universe). Each still teaches
one clean, transferable product lesson. Mostly triumphant/legendary arcs to fit the positive
house tone, each lesson distinct so the series never repeats.

1. **ChatGPT / OpenAI** — *The Interface That Changed Everything* — a simple interface can unlock
   capability the world couldn't reach before; distribution beats raw tech.
2. **Nvidia** — *How Nvidia Won the AI Gold Rush* — sell the shovels; build the platform everyone
   else has to build on (CUDA), and bet early on a future demand.
3. **SpaceX** — *The Rocket That Lands Itself* — first-principles thinking; attack the cost
   structure everyone treats as fixed (reusability).
4. **Dropbox** — *The Video That Built a Company* — validate demand before you build; the fake-door
   / demo MVP.
5. **Figma** — *Why Figma Beat Adobe* — make the product multiplayer; collaboration as a moat, on a
   hard technical bet (the browser).
6. **Slack** — *Born From a Dead Video Game* — the by-product becomes the product; the internal tool
   you built for yourself is the business.
7. **Stripe** — *7 Lines of Code* — obsess over your real user (the developer); remove friction until
   adoption is effortless.
8. **Canva** — *They Said No 100 Times* — democratize a hard skill for everyone; freemium + relentless
   persistence.
9. **Nintendo** — *How Nintendo Wins by Losing the Spec War* — blue ocean; don't out-power rivals,
   change the game you're playing.
10. **Robinhood** — *Free Trades and the GameStop Storm* — change the business model (free, monetize
    elsewhere) — and the double edge of making a serious thing feel like a game.

## House rules (same as series 1 & 2, plus the animation push)
- Cold open on the hook — the FIRST words. No generic intro. "I'm EV, from pmnorthstar" lands after.
- Teach ONE core product principle per video; name it, prove it with the company's real moves,
  generalize it so it transfers.
- Positive framing; name a hard moment only as setup for the win (Robinhood's ethics beat is the
  honest exception — surface the tension).
- Global audience: gloss any region-specific reference in one line.
- Land one interview takeaway per video ("in a case, you'd say…").
- Keep numbers directional, not invented.
- Target ~1,450–1,650 spoken words, brisk pace, fast transitions. Density is the feature.
- **Go big on visuals.** Write [VISUAL:] cues as ambitious, kinetic, cinematic set-pieces — this
  series is the showcase. Each script carries a "WHERE THEY ARE NOW (2025–26)" beat and a top-of-file
  **brand visual kit** (signature colors + logo motif + imagery) for the animation pass.
