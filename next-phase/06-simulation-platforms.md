## 1. Capsim (capsim.com)

### Products
| Product | Level | Description |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| **Capstone** | Advanced (MBA) | Full enterprise simulation — R&D, Marketing, Production, Finance, TQM, HR |
| **Foundation** | Intermediate | Simplified Capstone for bridging intro → advanced |
| **CapsimCore** | Entry-level | Forgiving sim for introductory courses, core business concepts |
| **CompXM** | Assessment | Individual exam — manage company alone + answer board queries (used for accreditation) |

### Pricing
- **$45–65/student** for single-semester license
- Course bundles with textbooks: ~$78

### What Students Actually Do
1. Analyze the **Courier Report** (industry newsletter with financials, market segments, perceptual maps, competitor data)
2. Make interconnected decisions across ALL departments simultaneously:
   - **R&D:** Product specs (performance, size, MTBF), new/retire products
   - **Marketing:** Price, promotion budget (awareness), sales budget (accessibility), demand forecast
   - **Production:** Units to produce, capacity buy/sell, automation level
   - **Finance:** Issue/retire stock, long-term bonds, short-term borrowing, dividends
   - **HR:** Recruiting spend, training hours, annual raise %
   - **TQM:** Quality initiatives (CPI, benchmarking)
3. Submit decisions → simulation processes ALL teams' inputs → new market state
4. Repeat for **8 rounds** (years)

### Scoring: Balanced Scorecard
| Category | Metrics |
|----------|---------|
| Financial (25%) | Stock price, profit, ROE, ROA |
| Customer (25%) | Market share, product count, SGA ratio |
| Internal Process (25%) | Contribution margins, utilization, inventory days |
| Learning & Growth (25%) | Employee productivity, asset turnover, revenue/employee |

### The Meta (How Winners Win)
- **TQM/HR hack:** Max out TQM and HR investments early → dramatically lower labor costs and turnover
- **Hit the "ideal spot":** Read the perceptual map religiously, keep R&D updated to match customer drift
- **Collaborative decisions:** Over-forecasting production → excess inventory → devastating emergency loans
- **Data over intuition:** Students who try to "game" the system fail; success requires deep analysis

### Common Fatal Mistakes
1. ☠️ Running out of cash → forced high-interest emergency loans
2. ☠️ Products drifting outside customer ideal spots on the perceptual map
3. ☠️ Siloed decisions (R&D designs product but Finance didn't fund capacity)
4. ☠️ Over-automating (can't pivot product specs quickly when market shifts)

## 2. GoPractice (gopractice.io)

### Products
- **Data-Driven Product Management** simulator
- **Product Growth** simulator
- **SQL Simulator**
- **AI/ML Simulators**

### How It Actually Works
- **Zero video lectures.** Entirely text-based + interactive.
- Built like a **"choose-your-own-adventure" RPG** — you role-play as a PM in a simulated tech company
- Given access to **simulated analytics dashboards** (like Amplitude)
- Must pull real data to:
  - Build funnels
  - Calculate LTV
  - Design A/B tests with confidence intervals
  - Build impact models
- Can't advance until you master the task

### Pricing
| Product | Price |
|---------|-------|
| AI/ML Simulator | ~$1,190 |
| Product Growth | ~$1,690 |
| Mini-simulators | ~$299 |

### Student Reviews
> *"It feels like real work experience. You can't advance until you master the task. It's time-intensive (1-1.5 months) and requires active problem-solving."*

### Relevance to northstar
GoPractice validates the **text + interactive RPG** format — which is essentially what SimulateIt already does. The difference: GoPractice focuses on analytics/data skills, northstar focuses on business/product decisions. And GoPractice costs $299-$1,690. **northstar is free.**

## 3. Markstrat (stratx-simulation.com)

### Versions
- B2C Durable Goods (the classic)
- B2C Consumer Goods (FMCG)
- B2B
- Digital Markstrat

### How It Works
- Teams of 3-6 act as marketing executives competing in a **closed market**
- Core metric: **Stock Price Index (SPI)**
- Teams allocate budgets across R&D, Marketing Mix, and Sales Forces
- Simulation runs on complex mathematical models reacting to every team's moves
- **8-10 periods** (years), highly analytical

### The Killer Mechanic
You have to **BUY market research**. Each study costs budget:
- Semantic scales (customer perceptions)
- Multi-dimensional scaling (MDS) maps
- Conjoint analysis
- Market forecasts

Teams that skip research fly blind. Teams that over-buy waste money. **The meta-game is deciding what information is worth paying for.**

### Pricing
Not publicly listed (B2B sales, request a quote). Generally $100+/student.

### How Winners Win
- **Buy market research every round** (non-negotiable)
- **Target one segment per brand** (don't try to please everyone)
- **Invest in R&D continuously** (customer preferences shift every round)
- Avoid price wars — differentiate instead

### Common Fatal Mistakes
1. ☠️ Under-investing in R&D → aging products get leapfrogged
2. ☠️ Positioning mismatch → advertising to segments that don't want your specs
3. ☠️ Price wars instead of differentiation
4. ☠️ Late entry into new product category (Vodites) → lose first-mover advantage

## 4. Harvard Business Publishing (HBSP) Simulations

### Popular MBA Simulations
| Simulation | Duration | Format | What Students Do |
|-----------|----------|--------|-----------------|
| **Universal Rental Car V2** | 90-120 min | Single session | Manage rental agency in Florida, optimize pricing between business/leisure travelers over 12 simulated months |
| **SimCase (Alishar)** | 10-15 min | Individual | Short digital exercises for quick decision-making |
| **Everest V3** | 90 min | Team | Lead a team to summit Everest — share info or hoard it? Push on or retreat? |
| **Back Bay Battery** | 90-120 min | Team | Incumbent facing disruptive tech — invest in disruption or protect cash cow? |

### Pricing
| Context | Price |
|---------|-------|
| Degree-granting courses | **~$11.50/student** |
| Corporate/non-degree | **$28.50–$49.00/student** |

### Key Mechanic
HBP sims focus on **single-session, high-pressure scenarios** with mid-simulation shocks. Market shocks are injected mid-game (competitor drops prices, demand shifts). Focus is on analytics and debrief, not just the score.

## 5. Brilliant.org

### Pedagogical Approach
**"First-principles active learning"** — they DON'T teach the concept first.

1. Present a simplified challenge/puzzle
2. Force the user to form a hypothesis and trial-and-error it
3. Provide progressive hints via AI tutor (Koji) — asks guiding questions, never gives the answer directly
4. THEN explain the concept after the user has struggled with it

### What Makes It Sticky
| Mechanic | How It Works |
|----------|-------------|
| **Bite-sized** | 15-minute lessons to manage cognitive load |
| **Playful interactives** | Drag-and-drop, sliders, animations, haptic feedback |
| **Daily streaks** | Streak counter with repair/freeze mechanics (loss-aversion hook) |
| **XP/Levels** | Points, level progression, badges, daily challenge rings |
| **AI Tutor (Koji)** | Socratic hints — guides reasoning without giving answers |
| **Push notifications** | Optimized for daily 15-min micro-habits |

### Pricing
- B2C: Monthly/Annual subscription
- B2B: Teacher & Parent progress dashboards

### Key Lesson for northstar
**Challenge first, concept after.** Don't explain the framework then quiz. Present the dilemma, let the student struggle, THEN reveal what the real company did and why. This is exactly what SimulateIt's "rationale reveal" already does — but Brilliant proves it's the pedagogically optimal approach.
